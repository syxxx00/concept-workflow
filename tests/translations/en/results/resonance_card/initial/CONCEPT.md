# Echo Workshop: Understanding for This Task

English reading translation of the [Chinese original](../../../../../results/resonance_card/initial/CONCEPT.md). The recorded run used Chinese materials; this translation is not a new test run.

The sources are the "Designer-Confirmed Notes," "Original Text of the Two Devices," and "Three Practice Records" in *Echo Workshop: Instructions for the Beginner Table*. This document organizes only this part of the gameplay. The relationships below are an organization of and deductions from the source, not new effects.

- **Charge currently held and pending gains.** Charge belongs to each individual player. A successful manual operation requires full payment first, followed by entry creation; an unexecuted gain cannot be used to pay. In Case A, the second operation is paid for using the 4 charge left after the first payment. Pending gains cannot be counted in advance.
- **The owner, the current operator, and the person recorded in an entry.** The owner determines where the card is stored; the current operator determines who may operate it now. The operator recorded at entry creation determines who "you" refers to when it is carried out. In Case C, Luo's ownership of the card does not change who receives the gain already recorded for Lan.
- **Successful operation and the echo condition.** Echo Plate responds only when another device operated by its current operator successfully adds a manual-operation entry to the pending list. At the time of the successful operation in Case C, Lan operates Resonator and Luo operates Echo Plate, so there is no echo. A later change of operator does not create it retroactively.
- **Whether the source is still available to operate, and whether existing entries continue.** Removing a device changes the conditions for new operations. It does not cancel entries already created. In Case B, Resonator returns to Luo's storage area, and both pending entries still give their gains to Luo.

An unspecified part of the scope: the notes explicitly place echo entries at the top and specify top-to-bottom execution, but do not specify where manual-operation entries are inserted. The rules document does not fill this gap with a fixed insertion position. All entries in the three records increase charge, so this missing detail does not change feasibility, gain attribution, or final charge. The two echoes in Case A are each placed at the top when created; the second does not replace the first.

The review for this task supports the handling of these three records. If later material changes the payment timing, echo condition, or entry attribution, first revise the relevant relationship, then check the operations and answers it affects.
